# Round table 4: Haven or Harbour, who it's for, and how much Fuji

> Simulated, like the earlier rounds: composite personas, not real people.

Same panel: **Noor** (UX designer), **Sam** (product manager), amateurs **Lea**, **Bram** and **Jules**, pros
**Femke**, **Karim** and **Anouk**.

Three questions from the owner:

1. Ferry, or something with *haven* / *harbour* ("a safe haven for your photos")?
2. Is this a Fujifilm problem, or a general one? Should the site be less about Fuji?
3. Would pros use it at all, or do they just pull the card or plug in a cable because it's faster?

## 3. Would pros use it?

Taken first, because the answer decides who the site talks to.

**Karim (wedding and events):** Honestly, no, not for the job itself. I come home with two cards of RAW files,
60 to 120 GB. A UHS-II reader does that in minutes. Over Wi-Fi the camera sends JPEGs only, a few per second at
best. I'd be there all night and I wouldn't have my RAWs.

**Femke (press and corporate):** There's one case. At an event the client wants five photos on LinkedIn before
the speeches end. I mark them on the camera, send them, and they're on the laptop without me opening the card
slot in a crowd. That's real, but it's a side use, and I already have an FTP setup on my newer body for it.

**Anouk (documentary, shoots a Fuji as second body):** I'd use it on the Fuji I carry on my days off. That's
the amateur in me, not the pro.

**Karim:** And a pro always has a reader. It's in every bag I own.

**Lea:** I *don't* have one. My laptop has two USB-C ports and no slot. The cable that came with the camera is
somewhere in a drawer. That's the whole reason I'd download this.

**Bram:** Same. I take 40 photos on a Sunday walk. I want them in Photos on Sunday evening, not next month when I
find the dongle.

**Jules:** And on holiday nobody packs a reader.

**Sam:** So the answer is clear: **pros are not the audience.** They have a faster, complete workflow (RAW, card
reader, ingest software), and we can't beat it over Wi-Fi. Our people are enthusiasts and families who shoot
JPEG or JPEG+RAW, take the camera on walks and trips, and don't have or don't want a reader. Pros are welcome for
the quick-selects case, and the FAQ should say honestly that a card reader is faster for a full card.

**Noor:** That also means the site should stop sounding like a tool. Less "ingest", more "your photos end up
where your other photos are".

## 2. Is it a Fujifilm problem?

**Sam:** It started as one: Fujifilm stopped maintaining Camera Remote, and there was no desktop app for its
Wi-Fi. But the pattern is the same everywhere. Every brand built its Wi-Fi for a **phone** app, and getting
photos to a **computer** without a cable is an afterthought or missing. Canon, Olympus, Sony and Panasonic
owners have the same problem.

**Anouk:** Then the site shouldn't open with Fujifilm. A Canon owner bounces in two seconds.

**Karim:** But don't oversell. Only the X-T20 is tested on a real camera. Everything else is the emulator or
"should work".

**Noor:** So: brand-neutral promise up front, honest support on the cameras page. "Works best with Fujifilm
today; other brands are experimental." The hero note about replacing Camera Remote moves down, to the cameras
block and the FAQ, where Fuji owners searching for it will still find it.

**Sam:** And the how-it-works steps shouldn't name Fuji menus. "Turn on the camera's Wi-Fi, the way you would
for its phone app." Fuji's PC AutoSave becomes "on Fujifilm cameras".

## 1. Ferry, Haven or Harbour

**Sam:** Same rules as round 3: short, says what happens, works in six languages.

**Anouk:** *Haven* is Dutch for harbour. For a Dutch app that's a nice wink, and English speakers read "safe
haven".

**Lea:** "A safe haven for your photos." I get that immediately. Ferry I had to think about.

**Jules:** `haven watch ~/Pictures` reads fine too.

**Femke:** *Harbour* has a problem: it's *Harbor* in American English. Half the people will spell it the other
way, and the domain and the App Store name have to pick one. Harbor is also a big open-source container
registry, so search is crowded.

**Bram:** Ferry is about the trip. Haven is about where the photos end up. For me the second is what I care
about: they're safe, they're with the others.

**Karim:** One warning: "safe haven" sounds like storage or backup. People might expect a cloud. The site has to
say clearly it brings photos *to your computer*, and doesn't keep them anywhere itself.

**Noor:** Agreed. The line is "bring your photos safely home", not "store your photos". Visually Haven keeps the
water and horizon of Ferry; the hero can stay a dusk over the water.

**Sam:** Known names to check: the Guardian Project's *Haven* (an Android security app) and a few companies
called Haven. Different category, but it goes on the trademark checklist with the domain and the stores.

## Decisions

- **Name: Haven** (working name). Tagline: *A safe haven for your photos.* Ferry is the runner-up, Harbour
  dropped (spelling split, crowded search).
- **Audience:** enthusiasts and families without a card reader at hand. Not pros. The FAQ says a card reader is
  faster for a full card of RAW files, and names the pro case it does help with: sending a few selects from
  the camera.
- **Positioning:** brand-neutral. Fujifilm moves from the hero to the cameras block, the FAQ and the cameras
  page, where support is stated honestly.
- **Copy:** "safely home", never "storage" or "backup service". It's always clear photos go to *your* computer.
